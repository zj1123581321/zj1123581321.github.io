## 1 Some Idle Words First

When I first conceived this 2019 review in my head, I had piles of things I wanted to say, but the moment I actually sat down to write, I no longer knew where to begin.

Fine — let me start with my WeChat Official Account (the main publishing platform in China). I'm sorry it went essentially unupdated over the last few months of 2019. The reasons are really just two: first, I was genuinely busy — a lot of things in the second half of the year converged at once, leaving no time to write for the account; second, plain laziness — putting words and pictures out in Official Account form carries a certain psychological weight of its own.

Most of the time I post in my [private Telegram channel](https://t.me/onenoteforzhanglixing). For one, Telegram's clients cover all platforms and make it convenient to share things straight from a phone; for another, TG is relatively free, and I never worry about some inexplicable "sensitive word" blocking the post. In the new year, though, I'll strive to guarantee at least one piece of output on the Official Account each month, using that to force myself to keep taking in input.

![](/migrated-images/markdown_20200116_g0NAUfyHUz7s.png)

In 2019, nothing changed about me outwardly, but cognitively, the first half of the year and the second were worlds apart.

Around the first half of 2019 I was still leaning idealistic, preoccupied all day with hyped-up concepts like AI and blockchain, listening every day to endless predictions about industry trends and rosy visions of the future. It looked as if I had gained a great deal of knowledge, but probe an inch deeper and it all felt hollow — grand, useless talk.

Some of it — certain industry-trend analyses, say — you can't call wrong, and for large companies it may even serve as directional guidance. But for a tiny individual like me, it has one fatal problem: none of it can be put into practice. For me, then, all of this is useful-sounding nonsense. Fine to skim; never to be taken too seriously. It's like how we all check the local weather when we head out, and nobody looks up the world's average temperature — it affects us far too little. What can genuinely be executed is what a tiny individual like me needs to pay attention to.

Perhaps because I noticed this problem, my information sources gradually shifted toward content about individual execution and cognition in the second half of the year. Along the way, I owe special thanks to a few mentors.

## 2 What Was Done and What Is to Be Done

### 2.1 What Was Done

1.  Started a WeChat Official Account; across all channels in the first half of the year I drew roughly a few thousand followers. On the corresponding Zhihu account (Zhihu: China's largest Q&A platform), 4k upvotes and 20k saves.
2.  Started making video content at the end of the year; one video uploaded so far, with 20k views across all platforms.
3.  A lost-and-found WeChat mini program (lightweight apps that run inside WeChat) is essentially finished.

Frankly, I still watched more than I did. My specific skill points may have improved somewhat, but measured against the volume of information absorbed, the conversion rate was low. So the new year has only one theme — **trial and error**.

### 2.2 Goals for 2020

1.  Launch another mini program, get daily active users to 1,000, and reach break-even. If conditions allow, try a combination of online and offline operation.
2.  Hatch a one-off business that does a thousand orders a month.
3.  Break 1,000,000 views across all platforms for the year, reach 1,000 followers on Bilibili (China's YouTube-like video community), and pass 10,000 views on YouTube.
4.  Score over 100 on the TOEFL...
5.  Get down to 130 jin (about 65 kg)...

If all goes smoothly, combining the mini program with the offline business will be the center of gravity for 2020 — it may not turn a profit, but the experience gained means no loss. The second goal is about leaving myself a way out, so I won't end up too strapped. The third is about claiming a spot during Bilibili's bonus window; in a couple of days I'll also write up some thoughts and suggestions on making video content right now — after all, being early is itself the advantage, and it must be seized. The last two items can't be put off any longer.

If the goals are all completed on time, there will be a big red envelope (hongbao — a traditional cash gift) for my readers.

### 2.3 Long-Term Items

1.  Learn SEO and hatch an English site with over 10,000 monthly unique visitors.
2.  Improve my logical rigor. I still lack critical-thinking ability: some articles feel subtly off to me, but I can't articulate where the problem lies. Missing logic is a fairly fatal flaw.
3.  Get better at telling stories. Wisecracking I can manage, but when it comes to telling a story, even the most entertaining one turns flavorless in my mouth.

That's all the holes I'll dig for now; I'll fill them slowly. The reason for choosing these things is simple: each either grows my experience, or takes one investment and pays back for life. After all, the greatest arbitrage opportunity is time.

## 3 Odds and Ends from 2019

Below are the pieces of content, data, and reflections that kept echoing in my head through 2019.

> When a wave arrives, the best move is not to throw yourself into the wave, but to provide tools for the wave.

Whether the people grinding for grad-school entrance exams, civil-service exams, or certificates achieved their ideals, I don't know — but I do know that plenty of people, from course production at the source, to online tutoring, all the way to the very end of the chain reselling courses, are living quite comfortably.

Whether Official Accounts made money is unclear, but the service providers supplying technical support to creators are doing pretty well.

> No matter how well known a thing is in China, there are 100 million people who don't know it — and often it's actually a billion who don't.

Information asymmetry exists forever, and information asymmetry is money. Two casual questions about shopping, if you'd like to test yourselves and see whether you know:

1.  Have you used 1688 (Alibaba's domestic wholesale marketplace)?
2.  Why are there dedicated websites, apps, and Official Accounts that send you coupons for Taobao products? Where do those coupons come from?

> 2019 will be the worst year of the past decade — and the best year of the next one.

By my eye, a whole wave of services that look able to cure anxiety is about to roll in and harvest. Hold the handrail, and be careful not to crash. The money of people who want to make money may be the easiest money there is :)

> Bilibili, 2019 Q3: 128 million monthly active users; 83 minutes of average daily use per user; 2.5 billion monthly user interactions

Compared with Xigua Video (ByteDance's video platform), I rate Bilibili as the more likely candidate to become China's YouTube. While Bilibili still needs to expand its long-tail content, find the right niche and get in fast — being early is the advantage.

## 4 Superlatives

### 4.1 Best-Value Electronic Product Bought in 2019

Early in 2019 I picked up a used iPad 2018 32G, and once it had basically solved my file-handling needs, my Surface Pro Pen essentially retired. On the hardware side, latency and offset can't match the iPad; on the software side, there's no iPad-class ecosystem. Apart from a somewhat better screen — the Surface Pro, alas.

Through the iPad plus its comprehensive software ecosystem, I basically achieved a paperless life. Later, during Pinduoduo's "10-Billion Subsidy" sales event, I even talked several friends around me into getting iPads, and wrote them a simple primer on iPad file management (I'll polish it and publish it in a few days).

For people who need to write often, the iPad deserves one word: worth it.

Pinduoduo, Bilibili, Meituan.

### 4.4 The Book I Most Want to Recommend from 2019

I didn't read much in 2019, and most of what I read was rather utilitarian in purpose. So let me recommend a novel read for no utilitarian reason at all — *Drawing Sword*. Yes, that *Drawing Sword*, the one with Li Yunlong. I've read the novel roughly three or four times: I know the ending perfectly well, yet I still tear up every time I finish it. The TV series covers only the first half of the novel; the content after that may be even more worth pondering.

You can read it for free with an unlimited card on WeRead (WeChat's e-reading app), or buy the print edition.
