## 1 A Bit of Rambling

When I was composing my 2019 summary in my head, I had a whole lot I wanted to say, but once it came time to actually write it down, I didn't know where to begin.

Fine, let me start with my WeChat official account. I'm sorry that it basically went without updates for the last few months of 2019. There are really just two reasons. First, I was genuinely busy: a lot of things piled up in the second half of the year, and I couldn't find the time to write. Second, naturally, I was lazy. Turning text and images into an official-account post still puts a certain amount of psychological pressure on me.

Most of the time I post in my [private Telegram channel](https://t.me/onenoteforzhanglixing). For one thing, Telegram has clients on every platform, so it's convenient, and I can share things straight from my phone. For another, Telegram is relatively free, and I don't have to worry about some random sensitive word keeping a post from going through. But in the new year, I'll try to make sure the official account gets at least one post a month, using output to force myself to take in more input.

![](/migrated-images/markdown_20200116_g0NAUfyHUz7s.png)

In 2019 I didn't change much in outward form, but in terms of how I see things, the person I was in the first half of the year and the person I was in the second half were worlds apart.

Around the first half of 2019, I was still rather idealistic. I spent my days following heavily hyped concepts like AI and blockchain. Every day I felt like I was hearing a great many predictions about industry trends and visions of the future, and it seemed as if I were gaining a lot of knowledge. But dig a little deeper and it all felt hollow — big talk that was of no practical use.

Some of this content, such as certain analyses of industry trends, you can't call wrong. For large companies, it may serve as some kind of guidance. But for a tiny individual like me, it has one fatal problem: there's no way to put it into practice. So to me, it's useful nonsense. Fine to glance at, but not to take too seriously. It's like how we check the local weather every day before going out, and nobody looks at the average global temperature — it affects us too little. What a tiny individual like me needs to pay attention to is what can actually be executed.

Perhaps because I noticed this problem, in the second half of the year my information sources gradually shifted toward content about individual execution and about cognition. In this process, I especially want to thank a few teachers for their help.

The first to thank is Teacher Senmiao. His execution and his thinking are both excellent, and over the past year I was lucky enough to watch how he carried an arbitrage project built on information gaps through to completion, step by step. I really admire it. Every day, Teacher Senmiao often shares high-quality excerpts, interesting people, and reflections in his Knowledge Planet group. Through him, I discovered many seniors with both deep thinking and hands-on experience in different fields, and I'm also putting into practice a small arbitrage project of my own. But however much I read, to me all of this can only count as information. What hasn't been thought through and internalized can hardly be called knowledge. So let me talk about what I did in 2019, and what I'm going to do in 2020.

## 2 What I Did and What I Will Do

### 2.1 What I did

1.  Launched my official account; in the first half of the year I drove traffic from all channels, reaching roughly a few thousand followers. On the corresponding Zhihu account: 4k likes and 20k bookmarks.
2.  At the end of the year I started making video content; so far I've uploaded one video, which has 20k views across all platforms.
3.  The lost-and-found mini program is basically finished.

Frankly, I still read a lot more than I did. I may have improved on some specific skills, but relative to the amount of information I took in, the conversion rate is very low. So the new year has only one theme — **trial and error**.

### 2.2 2020 tasks

1.  Launch another mini program, reach 1,000 daily active users, and break even. If conditions allow, try combining online and offline operations.
2.  Incubate a one-off business that sells a thousand orders a month.
3.  Hit 1 million total views across all platforms for the year, reach 1,000 followers on Bilibili, and get over 10k views on YouTube.
4.  Score above 100 on the TOEFL...
5.  Get down to 130...

If all goes well, combining the mini program with an offline business will be the focus of my work in 2020. It may not turn a profit, but gaining experience is no loss. The second task is to leave myself a way out, so I don't end up living too desperately. The third is to stake out a spot while Bilibili is still in its dividend period; in a couple of days I'll also write a post with some thoughts and advice on making video content right now, since being early is an advantage and I should seize it. As for the last two, I can't put them off any longer.

If I complete these tasks successfully, I'll give readers a big red envelope.

### 2.3 Long-term tasks

1.  Learn SEO and incubate an English-language site with over 10,000 monthly UV.
2.  Improve my logic. At the moment I still lack critical thinking; with some articles I vaguely feel something is off but can't say what the problem is. Lacking logic is a pretty fatal problem.
3.  Improve my storytelling. I'm fine at joking around, but when it comes to telling a story, even the most interesting one turns bland in my mouth.

That's all the holes I'll dig for now; I'll fill them in slowly. The reason I picked these things is simple: they either build up my experience, or they're a one-time investment with a lifetime of returns. After all, the biggest arbitrage opportunity is time.

## 3 Miscellaneous Thoughts on 2019

Here are some of the ideas, figures, and reflections that kept echoing in my head throughout 2019.

> When a wave arrives, the best thing to do is not to throw yourself into it, but to provide tools for the wave.

I don't know whether those preparing for graduate entrance exams, civil service exams, or certification exams achieved their dreams, but I do know that from course production at the source, to online tutoring, to those at the very end who resell courses a second time, quite a few people are living comfortably.

I'm not sure whether official accounts made money, but the service providers offering technical support to creators are doing quite well.

> No matter how well known something is in China, there are a hundred million people who don't know it — but often it's a billion people who don't.

Information gaps always exist, and information gaps are money. Take shopping: here are two questions, and you can test whether you know the answers.

1.  Have you used 1688?
2.  Why are there dedicated websites, apps, and official accounts that give you coupons for Taobao products? Where do those coupons come from?

> 2019 will be the worst year of the past decade, and the best year of the next decade.

I expect a whole batch of services that appear to relieve anxiety to come out and reap the harvest. Hold on to the handrail and be careful not to tip over. Making money off people who want to make money is perhaps the easiest of all :)

> Bilibili, 2019 Q3: 128 million monthly active users; average daily time spent per user of 83 minutes; 2.5 billion monthly user interactions

Compared with Xigua Video, I think Bilibili is more likely to become China's YouTube. While Bilibili still needs to expand its long-tail content, find your niche and get in quickly — being early is an advantage.

> [Ou Chengxiao: There is only one answer to retirement](https://mp.weixin.qq.com/s/S6Nnx1lFlevXfRO3BeHVsQ)

Nothing more to say. Just hustle.

## 4 Lists

### 4.1 The most worthwhile electronic product I bought in 2019

At the start of 2019 I picked up a used 2018 iPad with 32 GB. Once it had basically solved my file-handling needs, my Surface Pro Pen was more or less retired. On the hardware side, the latency and offset are worse than the iPad's, and on the software side it has no ecosystem like the iPad's. Apart from the nicer screen, the Surface Pro, sigh.

With the iPad and its comprehensive software ecosystem, I've basically achieved a paperless life. Later, during Pinduoduo's 10-billion-yuan subsidy campaign, I even talked several friends around me into buying iPads, and wrote them a simple beginner's guide to managing files on the iPad (I'll polish it and publish it in a couple of days).

For anyone who writes by hand often, the iPad comes down to one phrase: so good.
