WeChat recently released its 2018 data report. The report states that [as of September 2018, WeChat's monthly active users had reached 1.082 billion](https://awtmt.com/articles/3466022?from=wscn).

Although open rates for WeChat Official Accounts (the main publishing platform in China) have kept falling in recent years, one thing is undeniable: WeChat's enormous user base means the vast majority of content producers cannot afford to abandon this battlefield.

According to the data in the [2017 WeChat Economic Data Report](http://www.sohu.com/a/203492754_481775) and the [2017 WeChat User Research and Business Opportunity Insights](http://www.199it.com/archives/585401.html), by the end of 2017 WeChat Official Accounts numbered over 10 million, of which 3.5 million were active — up 14% from 2016 — with 797 million monthly active followers.

Naturally, WeChat's ecosystem holds a great many high-quality content producers, who put out a wealth of valuable material.

So, when we read a brilliant article, how should we save it? WeChat Favorites?

My advice: save it however you like, but **don't use "WeChat Favorites"**.

## 1. Why I Don't Recommend "WeChat Favorites"

### 1.1 It Saves Only the Link, Not the Content

Most of the time this is fine, but sometimes, for all sorts of reasons, **an article gets blocked by WeChat, or deleted by its author**. When that happens, the item still sits in your favorites list, but you can no longer view its content — at which point saving it has lost all meaning.

![Deleted by the author](/migrated-images/markdown_20190516_dQX98g3iBLdd.png)

![Content blocked](/migrated-images/markdown_20190516_G5ModNhFnp0u.png)

### 1.2 Poor Cross-Platform Support

For an article worth keeping that you read on your phone, you can perhaps still "send it to WeChat Favorites." But on a computer, saving articles into WeChat Favorites is nowhere near as convenient — you can't very well copy-paste back and forth.

So how do we truly save an article, in the real sense of the word? I recommend a third-party cloud notebook.

## 2. Using a Third-Party Cloud Notebook

### 2.1 Why Cloud-Notebook Saves Don't Fear Blocking or Deletion of the Original

WeChat Favorites records only the URL and stores no content. But third-party cloud notebooks typically save both the content and the URL. **Even if the article gets blocked by WeChat, you can still view the content you saved with the third-party cloud notebook.**

If an article is a house, then WeChat Favorites keeps only the key (the URL), so once the house is demolished (deleted/blocked), the key means nothing. The save feature of a third-party cloud notebook, by contrast, builds an identical house next door — so even when the original house is gone, we can still see what it looked like.

### 2.2 The Advantages of a Cloud Notebook

#### 2.2.1 You Can Clip Content

Already explained clearly in the principle above: this is saving in the true sense. The article's actual content is stored in your cloud notebook, easy to view later.

#### 2.2.2 Cross-Platform, with Web Clipping

-   **WeChat**: supports one-tap send to the cloud notebook
-   **Desktop browsers**: supports one-click web clipping
-   **Apps**: support sharing into the cloud notebook app

![One-tap send](/migrated-images/markdown_20190516_Y8XTDWpBHicy.jpg)

![OneNote clipping a web page](/migrated-images/markdown_20190516_xH5sc2lIEyoc.png)

#### 2.2.3 Strong Searchability

Because the article's content has been saved into our own notebook, even if we later forget the article's title, we can still quickly locate it by searching a few scattered words.

#### 2.2.4 A Relatively Complete Ecosystem

Professional cloud-notebook platforms generally partner with many other platforms, optimize specifically for different content platforms, and can interoperate with other tools. You can even use [IFTTT](https://ifttt.com/), the sort of automation tool, to unlock more possibilities.

### 2.3 How to Choose a Cloud-Notebook Platform

The two mainstream note apps internationally: Evernote and OneNote.

#### 2.3.1 Evernote (Yinxiang Biji)

Evernote is an app focused on note-taking. Yinxiang Biji is its China-market edition; the two keep **separate accounts** (they don't share a database), and Yinxiang Biji has a few extra localized features that Evernote lacks, such as saving content from WeChat and Weibo (China's Twitter-like platform).

**Pros**

-   Works across platforms (Windows, Mac, Android, iOS, Chrome, Firefox, and more)
-   High third-party service support; interoperates with many platforms (the best of the three)
-   **Can save WeChat and Weibo content (Yinxiang Biji)**
-   One-click web clipping that preserves formatting (Yinxiang Biji's web clipping utterly outclasses OneNote — never mind Youdao Cloud Notes)

**Limitations**

-   Free accounts don't support offline viewing and editing of notes. Notes can sometimes be accessed from cache, but this may cause sync conflicts.
-   Free accounts can sync only 2 devices.

![Yinxiang Biji pricing](/migrated-images/markdown_20190516_AU56Nwn2MmGB.png)

#### 2.3.2 OneNote (Microsoft's Notebook)

OneNote may well be the least-known app in the Office suite. Made by Microsoft.

**Pros**

-   Works across platforms (Windows, Mac, Android, iOS, Chrome, Firefox, and more)
-   Backed by Office, with excellent editing; easy integration with the Office suite
-   Fairly good third-party service support, though not as good as Yinxiang Biji
-   **Can save WeChat and Weibo content**
-   One-click web clipping, but it loses some formatting information (it just looks rather ugly)

**Limitations**

-   Free users get only 5 GB of cloud storage; you need an Office 365 subscription to expand it
-   On Windows, OneNote 2016 keeps hitting inexplicable sync bugs; other platforms are fine
-   The web version is currently inaccessible from mainland China

![OneNote UWP version](/migrated-images/markdown_20190516_vLrwG0LPrma5.png)

#### 2.3.3 More on Evernote vs. OneNote

Evernote and OneNote each have strengths and weaknesses. Even though I'm a heavy OneNote user, I deeply envy Yinxiang Biji's powerful clipping and third-party service support. But since I take handwritten notes on a Surface day to day, and subscribe to Office 365, I've stayed on the OneNote platform all along.

## 3. How to Read Blocked Articles

The principle is actually simple: **anything of value will surely have been saved by someone who cares.**

Copy the WeChat article's title and search for it with a search engine (**WeChat's built-in search actually works fine**). (Some articles may require Google to turn up.)

An example.

Banfo Xianren (Half-Buddha Immortal; Official Account ID: banfoSB) once published on his Official Account a 100k+ article, "The Death of Tuandai.com: A Brief History of the Loan Sharks' Annihilation." The article was brilliantly written, but for various reasons it was deleted.

Using WeChat search, type "the death of Tuandai."

![The results](/migrated-images/markdown_20190516_gNWdRXLmtuOc.jpg)

Then save it to your own cloud notebook platform in one tap, and the article is preserved for good.
