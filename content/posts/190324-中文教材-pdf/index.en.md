Why did the "Mastering PDF series" need to exist?

First, with the gradual rise of the iPad and the Surface, more and more people have chosen a digital way of studying. But many either can't find digital study materials, or the materials they find are of low quality — which greatly **damages the efficiency of digital learning**.

That property has also made PDF the publishing format of choice for the vast majority of textbooks, reports, and papers. Yet many people know very little about PDF and don't know how to handle common PDF-related problems.

## 1. Background on Chinese E-Textbooks

The digitization of Chinese textbooks lags behind that of English ones. English textbooks are mostly exported directly from source documents, while Chinese textbooks are mostly produced by scanning and photocopying the finished printed book. That's why the same book's English PDF may be only a few MB, while its Chinese translation can balloon into a PDF of hundreds of MB. And a scanned PDF that hasn't been through OCR doesn't support text operations at all.

Most Chinese PDF textbooks on the market come from one of four sources — **scans leaked from libraries, personal scans of books uploaded by individuals, open-source books (rare), and publisher files that leaked (rare)**.

Once you know the sources, you can apply the right remedy. Here's how to search. 👀

PS: If you find a textbook in Word, mobi, or Epub format, you can convert it to PDF with the relevant software — for the specifics, see [Can't tell mobi / epub / azw3 book formats apart?](https://mp.weixin.qq.com/s/0Ffajimw0UEqF7FS1pxXcg) (in Chinese).

## 2. How to Get Chinese PDF Textbooks

### 2.1 Netdisk Search Engines

File sharing inside China mostly happens on "netdisks" (cloud storage drives), and textbooks are no exception. Netdisks here include not only **Baidu Netdisk** (China's dominant cloud storage service) but also **Weibo Disk (by Sina), Lanzou Cloud, Chengtong Netdisk** and a whole crowd of other cloud storage providers.

But most of these netdisks provide no search function — "search" here means searching **content that was not shared publicly**. Take Baidu Netdisk as an example: public shares are directly searchable. But for shares protected by an extraction code, the official service offers no such search entry point.

That gap spawned a large batch of netdisk search engines, such as [CiliMao](https://www.cilimao.cc/) and [Xilinjie](http://www.xilinjie.com/). There are many more such sites — one Google search turns up piles of them.

### 2.3 Buying from People Who Have Access

#### 2.3.1 Check Whether a Library Has Scanned It, and at What Quality

As mentioned above, part of the source of Chinese PDF textbooks is scans leaked from libraries, so finding someone with library admin privileges can also get you the relevant PDF books.

![mark](/migrated-images/markdown_20190324_X13RxyVEiAj4.png)

First, search for the book on the [National Library Reference Alliance](http://www.ucdrs.superlib.net/) (a nationwide interlibrary document-delivery service in China); you can search by title, ISBN, author, and many other keys (ISBN search is recommended — it's extremely efficient).

PS: The ISBN is the number at the barcode in the lower-right corner of the book's back cover; you can also find it on the product page of online bookstores.

![mark](/migrated-images/markdown_20190324_c5fOJuC9Mit7.png)

If the [book's detail page](http://t.cn/EJUWhDk) has a **preview** (copyright page, preface, table of contents, body text) in the upper-right corner, you can purchase through this channel. **If there is no preview, you cannot purchase through this channel.**

Click in and check the clarity — **the clarity of the preview is exactly the clarity of the PDF textbook that gets produced**.

![mark](/migrated-images/markdown_20190324_pIbIJXaCK1Yq.png)

#### 2.3.2 Finding Someone to Buy From

You can find such sellers through Taobao and similar channels; the price is typically 5–15 yuan (roughly $1–2) per book, and you can pick your own.

To avoid any appearance of advertising, I won't make recommendations in this post. But if you reply "**e-book purchase**" (「电子书购买」) in the backend of my account, you'll get the contact details of one practitioner I recommend, at about 3 yuan (roughly $0.5) per book.

PS: Library scans come in the PDG format (a proprietary scan format used by Chinese library systems); converting to PDF takes some time, so please be patient.

### 2.4 General Search Engines

If you've made it to this step, either your book is fairly new and libraries haven't gotten around to scanning it, or the book is fairly obscure with little demand.

So now you have to rely on the broad masses of netizens: search with general search engines. Google is recommended, or one of its [mirror sites](https://coderschool.cn/1853.html) (relevant because Google itself is blocked in mainland China).

Of course, searching has its own craft.

#### 2.4.1 Make Good Use of "filetype:"

![mark](/migrated-images/markdown_20190324_oV1lkzF9OMNI.png)

**Note**: **the colon must be the English (half-width) colon.**

`filetype` restricts results to a single file type, e.g. .doc .pdf .xls .ppt .txt

#### 2.4.2 Make Good Use of English Double Quotes ""

![mark](/migrated-images/markdown_20190324_4BDSEXtbj4wR.png)

Content inside double quotes is **matched exactly** — that is, the results are guaranteed to contain exactly the content inside the quotes. Very handy when searching among many similar books.

#### 2.4.3 Make Good Use of "-"

![mark](/migrated-images/markdown_20190324_TiTrd3my8uy2.png)

The minus sign is usually used to **exclude a search term**; the screenshot shows results for machine learning excluding python.

**Note: there must be a space between the preceding keyword and the minus sign, and no space between the minus sign and the following keyword.**

#### 2.4.4 More Search Syntax Tricks

There are many more search syntax tricks; I won't expand here. You can refer to [this article](https://www.w3h5.com/post/220.html) (in Chinese) to learn more. Honestly, knowing just a few of the common ones will greatly improve your search efficiency.

PS: Most search engines today are **keyword-based** rather than semantic, so please don't type "how to find the book 笨方法学Python (Learn Python the Hard Way)" 🙃🙃🙃 — split it into keywords instead: "笨方法 python".

### 2.6 Crowdfunded Scanning

If you've tried all the methods above and still ended up here, congratulations — you're about to unlock a new skill. 😬

Please scan it yourself, then...

Buy the book on Taobao → mail it to a Taobao scanning shop → receive the PDF.

Typical quote:

> Quote: scan with the binding cut, 0.1 yuan/page; scan without cutting, 0.2 yuan/page. A3 scans require cutting the binding, 0.2 yuan/page. If your book has 300 pages, cut-and-scan costs 30 yuan (≈ $0.014/$0.028 per page; a 300-page book ≈ $4).

400 dpi is clarity enough; having the binding cut is recommended — the results are very good.

If it's a genuine need, I strongly suggest **starting a crowdfunded scan** for the book to split the cost. Because a slightly thicker book might sell for 15 yuan, but getting it scanned and produced as a PDF can run past 100 yuan for the full service.

You can join the "**E-Textbook Project**" (「电子教材计划」) described below to post your crowdfunding request (first judge for yourself whether the demand is niche enough).

## 3. Advanced Techniques

### 3.1 On Removing Baidu Netdisk's Speed Limit

![mark](/migrated-images/markdown_20190324_ijecEGV84mbe.png)

GitHub has many projects for lifting Baidu Netdisk's speed limit, such as [ProxyeeDown](https://github.com/proxyee-down-org/proxyee-down); you can search around yourself.

There are also some relatively mature third-party clients on the market, such as [SpeedPan](https://www.speedpan.com/) and [PanDownload](https://pandownload.com/).

Of course, all third-party clients carry risk — your account may end up throttled.

You can also use IDM (Internet Download Manager) plus Tampermonkey userscripts, which is a bit safer. If you can't be bothered to tinker, here is [a method from 奶酪 (Nailuo)](https://mp.weixin.qq.com/s/q-hCL2Xm4AFb3vINDDw7gQ) (in Chinese).
